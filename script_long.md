# THE BORING MONEY SYSTEM

**Topic:** Personal finance — why systems beat willpower
**Style:** Human-first documentary / Vox deep dive
**Format:** Landscape 16:9
**Story Strategy:** Mystery + character contrast + escalating failure + earned reveal
**Target Audience:** People who want to get better with money but keep changing strategies
**Goal:** Make the viewer follow Alex and Jordan through one question: why does one financial system survive real life while the other keeps collapsing?

## 1. STORY ENGINE

This version is built around a single narrative question rather than a list of rules:

> **Why does the smarter-looking money strategy keep losing to the boring one?**

The viewer meets two people with the same starting point. Alex keeps optimizing. Jordan removes decisions. The story lets the viewer predict who will win, then repeatedly tests that prediction.

Retention design:

- Beat 1: concrete mystery and immediate stakes.
- Beat 2: establish Alex and Jordan before explaining the lesson.
- Beat 3: first unexpected clue — Jordan makes fewer decisions.
- Beat 4: open the first major loop: what did Jordan automate?
- Beat 5: reveal the hidden enemy: decision fatigue and lifestyle creep.
- Beat 6: first rule, but immediately create a new question.
- Beat 7: second rule and an apparent failure.
- Beat 8: third rule as the answer to the failure, not as a lecture item.
- Beat 9: real-life stress test.
- Beat 10: evidence / illustrative math.
- Beat 11: reversal — the system is not about being disciplined.
- Beat 12: the tiny monthly routine that makes the system survive.
- Beat 13: emotional resolution.
- Beat 14: final meaning shift.
- Beat 15: one natural audience question, not a generic CTA.

Principles used: early clarity, unresolved questions, character identification, causal escalation, contrast, prediction, surprise, consequence, and a payoff that changes the meaning of the opening claim.
