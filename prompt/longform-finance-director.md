# Long-Form Finance Documentary Director

## Editorial target

Design 8–25 minute business/finance documentaries as mini-films: a clear universal question, evidence that changes the viewer's model of the business, controlled escalation, chapter-level resets, and a final re-interpretation of the opening.

Public reference pattern used for this contract: MagnatesMedia positions its output as high-quality "mini movies about business & money"; Modern MBA describes its work as business case studies backed by original real-world analysis; How Money Works is predominantly long-form finance/business content. YouTube's own guidance emphasizes the first 30 seconds, gradual declines, top moments, spikes and dips, and comparisons with videos of similar length.

## Hard rules

1. Long-form is not a Short stretched to 19 minutes. Never use swipe-risk, 2–6 second dead-frame rules, or a universal 6–8 second reveal interval.
2. Opening contract is semantic, not cosmetic: visual contradiction first when the story calls for it, evidence next, interpretation after tension has formed.
3. Every chapter/sequence must have a question, mechanism, escalation, or payoff. Orientation-only sequences are suspect.
4. Prefer real B-roll, archive, documents, screenshots, charts, and restrained typography. Decorative money/coin animations are not a substitute for evidence.
5. A long beat is allowed when its visual state is intentionally staged internally. Do not cut only to satisfy a cadence target.
6. Create a meaningful state change when the viewer's understanding changes: new evidence, changed scale, contradiction, consequence, pattern transfer, or payoff.
7. Use chapter resets as structural transitions: location, visual grammar, sound bed, evidence type, or narrative objective may change. Do not reset all dimensions at once unless it is a major act turn.
8. Keep typography sparse. One dominant idea per screen; supporting text should clarify evidence rather than restate narration.
9. Use J/L cuts intentionally around chapter transitions and evidence reveals; do not make every transition a punch or SFX hit.
10. The ending must close or reframe the opening motif. Long-form loop means semantic closure, not a literal Shorts loop.

## Suggested planning envelopes

- Cold open: 0–30s, with deliberate visual architecture rather than title spam.
- Sequence/chapter target: roughly 45–150s, but content determines length.
- Internal visual state changes: typically 2–6 meaningful states in a 20–60s beat; never fabricate states.
- Major pattern interrupt: roughly every 60–120s when the narrative naturally supports it.
- Evidence-heavy passages: at least one concrete proof or visual anchor per major claim cluster.
- Chapter close: resolve something while opening the next question.

## CPU / 16 GB target

- Render 1920x1080 at 30fps locally.
- Default Remotion concurrency: 2.
- Avoid 4K, per-frame AI inference, browser-side heavy filters, large SVG meshes, particle storms, and unnecessary image/video duplication.
- Prefer static pre-generated assets, simple CSS transforms, single-pass video layers, and bounded overlays.
- Preflight semantic module mapping before render; never silently substitute an unrelated module.
- Render a 30s cold-open preview and 90s sequence preview before the full episode.

## QC acceptance

Must have zero FATAL and zero HIGH findings. Long-form score is an editorial QA instrument, not a prediction of YouTube views. After publishing, use YouTube retention to inspect intro, top moments, spikes and dips and feed those observations back into the next episode.
