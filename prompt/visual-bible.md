# VISUAL BIBLE — PRODUCTION DEFAULT

This is the default art-direction contract for asset planning. A story-specific file may override it deliberately.

## WORLD

The asset pack should feel like one editorial film, but **medium is chosen by information type**. Photography, documents, cutouts, charts, maps, UI, and B-roll can coexist.

## COLOR

Start from a restrained documentary palette:

- charcoal / near-black for depth
- warm off-white / paper neutrals for neutral surfaces
- one story accent color
- natural skin / material colors when photorealism is required

Do not force the accent into every asset. It is a hierarchy tool, not a mandatory paint color.

## PHOTOGRAPHIC LANGUAGE

Use realistic exposure, physically plausible shadows, natural texture, controlled contrast, subtle grain, believable lenses, and period-accurate details. Avoid synthetic HDR, plastic CGI materials, over-smoothed skin, and fake cinematic haze.

## CUTOUT LANGUAGE

Foreground cutouts should have:

- clean segmentation
- no halo
- no matte contamination
- subtle contact shadow only when requested
- enough transparent padding for motion
- consistent viewpoint within a related asset family

## DOCUMENT LANGUAGE

Documents should look handled and printed, not like a flat UI card. Specify paper stock, print method, age, folds, stamps, handwriting, margins, and camera angle when those matter.

## GRAPHIC LANGUAGE

Charts and information graphics should be editorial and legible, with one visual idea per asset. Avoid decorative complexity that competes with the narration.

## B-ROLL LANGUAGE

Prefer documentary-commercial realism: motivated movement, credible physics, real materials, and useful composition. Shots must be independently reusable in a 1–2 second cut.

## COMPOSITING

Every foreground asset should be designed to coexist with the backgrounds specified for the same visual-bible group. Match light direction and color temperature where the asset will be composited.

## SAFE ZONE

Unless the editor specifies otherwise, protect the central 60% of the frame and leave practical caption space. Do not reserve huge dead bands unless the actual typography layout requires them.

## STYLE OVERRIDE RULE

The story may explicitly choose a stronger style—e.g. archival monochrome, paper-cut, blueprint, luxury editorial, retro computer interface, or illustrated explainer. That style must be declared as a **story-level override** rather than accidentally leaking from a previous episode.
